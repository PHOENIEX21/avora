## Section A: Comprehension (10 Questions, from passage below)

**Passage:**
"The debate over social media's impact on adolescent mental health has intensified in recent years. Proponents argue that these platforms offer valuable spaces for self-expression, community-building, and access to information that might otherwise be unavailable to young people, particularly in underserved areas. Critics, however, point to a growing body of research linking excessive social media use to increased anxiety, depression, and disrupted sleep patterns among teenagers. Some psychologists suggest that the issue is not social media itself, but rather the manner and duration of its use — passive scrolling through curated, idealized content appears more harmful than active engagement with genuine friends and communities. As policymakers grapple with potential regulations, parents and educators are left navigating this complex terrain largely on their own, often relying on inconsistent guidance."

**1.** What is the central debate discussed in this passage?
*Answer: Whether social media has a positive or negative impact on adolescent mental health.*

**2.** What benefit do proponents claim social media offers?
*Answer: Valuable spaces for self-expression, community-building, and access to information.*

**3.** What do critics link to excessive social media use?
*Answer: Increased anxiety, depression, and disrupted sleep patterns.*

**4.** According to some psychologists, what actually matters more than social media itself?
*Answer: The manner and duration of use (passive scrolling vs active engagement).*

**5.** Using context clues, what does "curated" most likely mean in this passage?
*Answer: Carefully selected/arranged (to present an idealized version) — inferred from its pairing with "idealized content."*

**6.** Is the sentence "these platforms offer valuable spaces for self-expression" presented as fact or opinion in this passage?
*Answer: Opinion — it is explicitly attributed to "proponents," a specific group's viewpoint, not stated as an established fact by the writer.*

**7.** What is left unresolved, according to the final sentence?
*Answer: Parents and educators are left navigating the issue largely on their own, without consistent guidance, while policymakers consider regulations.*

**8.** What is the writer's likely PURPOSE in this passage?
*Answer: To inform readers of a genuinely contested, multi-sided debate — presenting both proponent and critic views without taking a clear side.*

**9.** SKIM: state the passage's general topic in one sentence.
*Answer: The passage discusses the ongoing, unresolved debate about social media's effects on teenage mental health.*

**10.** SCAN: what specific sleep-related effect is mentioned?
*Answer: Disrupted sleep patterns.*

## Section B: Vocabulary and Lexis (10 Questions)

**11.** Add the correct prefix to reverse the meaning: "___connect" (using "dis-")
*Answer: disconnect.*

**12.** Form a noun from "govern" using "-ment," and explain how it differs from "governor."
*Answer: government (the system/institution); governor (the person who governs) — same root, different suffix, different meaning.*

**13.** Choose the antonym of "excessive": (moderate/extreme/plentiful)
*Answer: moderate.*

**14.** Identify the figure of speech: "The classroom was a zoo."
*Answer: Metaphor.*

**15.** Identify the figure of speech: "He runs as fast as a cheetah."
*Answer: Simile.*

**16.** Identify the figure of speech: "It's ironic that the fire station burned down."
*Answer: Irony.*

**17.** Identify the figure of speech: "She passed away peacefully."
*Answer: Euphemism.*

**18.** Choose the correct article: "She is (a/an) European visitor."
*Answer: a — "European" starts with a consonant SOUND (/j/, "yer-"), despite the vowel letter.*

**19.** What is the plural of "sheep"?
*Answer: sheep (unchanged).*

**20.** Give the meaning of "prehistoric" by breaking it into its prefix and root.
*Answer: "pre-" (before) + "historic" → before recorded history.*

## Section C: Grammar (20 Questions)

**21.** Choose the correct modal: "It's the law — you ___ wear a seatbelt." (must/have to — explain which fits an EXTERNAL rule)
*Answer: have to (though "must" is also acceptable when the SPEAKER is personally insisting; "have to" more precisely signals an external/legal rule).*

**22.** Correct: "Despite of the rain, we continued." Identify the double-error.
*Answer: "Despite the rain" OR "In spite of the rain" — combining "despite" and "of" is a common overcorrection error.*

**23.** Fill in the correct preposition: "She is worried ___ her exam results."
*Answer: about.*

**24.** Correct the comma splice: "She was tired, she went to bed early."
*Answer: "She was tired, so she went to bed early." (or use a semicolon or separate sentences)*

**25.** Complete the tense: "By the time the teacher arrived, the students ___ (finish) the test." (past perfect)
*Answer: had finished.*

**26.** Identify the tense: "She has finished her homework."
*Answer: Present perfect.*

**27.** Explain the difference in meaning between "He ate his food" and "He has eaten his food."
*Answer: Simple past states a completed past action with no strong link to now; present perfect emphasizes the action's relevance to the present moment.*

**28.** Correct: "She does not plays tennis."
*Answer: "She does not play tennis."*

**29.** Fill in "although" or "whereas": "Lagos is crowded, ___ Kano is comparatively quieter."
*Answer: whereas (a neutral comparison, not an unexpected contrast).*

**30.** Convert to passive: "The students are writing their exams."
*Answer: "The exams are being written by the students."*

**31.** Identify the adverbial type: "He was late because the bus broke down." (reason/purpose/condition)
*Answer: Reason.*

**32.** Place the frequency adverb correctly: "(never) She is on time."
*Answer: "She is never on time." (after the "be" verb)*

**33.** Correct: "They rarely are absent."
*Answer: "They are rarely absent."*

**34.** Choose the correct exception phrase: "___ his stubbornness, he would have accepted the compromise." (But for/Except)
*Answer: But for.*

**35.** Identify whether "become" is an action or state verb.
*Answer: State — it describes a change of condition, not a physical action being performed.*

**36.** Correct: "I am angry about my sister for breaking my phone." (identify the preposition error)
*Answer: "I am angry WITH my sister for breaking my phone." ("with" for a person, "about" for a situation)*

**37.** Insert the correct punctuation: "I need the following apples bread and milk" (needs a colon and commas)
*Answer: "I need the following: apples, bread, and milk."*

**38.** Correct: "The teacher's are meeting in the hall."
*Answer: "The teachers are meeting in the hall." (plain plural needs no apostrophe)*

**39.** Identify the correctly formed superlative: "This is the (bestest/best/most best) solution."
*Answer: best.*

**40.** Explain why "Whereas it was raining, she went out" sounds awkward, and correct it.
*Answer: "Whereas" doesn't carry the "despite/surprisingly" sense the sentence needs; it should be "Although it was raining, she went out."*

## Topic 1: Whole Numbers — Place Value to Millions/Billions, Standard Form (Introductory)

**Step 1 — Extend Primary 5's place value work to millions, using the same grouping-in-3s method already established**

**Worked Example:**
Read 4,250,000: group as 4 | 250 | 000 → four million, two hundred and fifty thousand.

**Step 2 — Introduce standard form as a preview (fully developed later in JSS, but the CONCEPT starts here)**

Standard form writes a number as (a number 1-10) × a power of 10. 4,000,000 = 4×10⁶ (since we move the decimal 6 places).

**Practice Questions:**
1. Read 7,300,000 in words.
2. Write 3,000,000 in standard form.

## Topic 2: Approximation and Estimation

**Step 1 — Recall the rounding rule (5 rounds up) and apply it at larger scales**

**Worked Example:**
Round 456,789 to the nearest ten-thousand. Look at the thousands digit (6): since 6≥5, round the ten-thousands digit up: 460,000.

**Practice Questions:**
1. Round 82,349 to the nearest thousand.
2. Estimate the total cost of 3 items priced ₦1,980, ₦2,950, and ₦4,120 by first rounding each to the nearest thousand.

## Topic 3: Factors, Multiples, LCM and HCF (Full Method)

**Step 1 — Recall factors/multiples from Primary 5, now applying the full LCM/HCF method (listing AND prime factorization)**

**Worked Example (LCM):**
Find the LCM of 8 and 12. Multiples of 8: 8,16,24... Multiples of 12: 12,24... First common: 24.

**Worked Example (HCF):**
Find the HCF of 18 and 24. Factors of 18: 1,2,3,6,9,18. Factors of 24: 1,2,3,4,6,8,12,24. Common: 1,2,3,6. Highest: 6.

**Practice Questions:**
1. Find the LCM of 6 and 9.
2. Find the HCF of 20 and 30.
3. Two bells ring every 6 and 8 minutes. When will they next ring together? (an LCM word problem)

## Topic 4: Fractions — Full Operations and Word Problems

**Step 1 — Consolidate all four operations, then apply to multi-step word problems**

**Worked Example (word problem combining operations):**
A tank is ⅔ full. If ¼ of the remaining space is filled, what fraction of the tank is now full?
Remaining space initially = 1−⅔=⅓. ¼ of that remaining space is filled: ¼×⅓=1/12. New total filled = ⅔+1/12. Common denominator=12: ⅔=8/12. 8/12+1/12=9/12=¾.

**Practice Questions:**
1. Add: 2⅓ + 1¾
2. A trader sold ⅗ of his oranges in the morning and ⅙ of the remainder in the afternoon. What fraction of the total did he sell in the afternoon? (careful: ⅙ of the REMAINDER, not of the original total)

## Topic 5: Ratio and Proportion (Introductory)

**Step 1 — Establish ratio as comparing quantities directly, connecting to the sharing method**

**Worked Example:**
Share 60 sweets between two children in the ratio 2:3.
Total parts=5. Each part=60÷5=12. First share=2×12=24. Second share=3×12=36.

**Practice Questions:**
1. Share ₦800 between two people in the ratio 3:5.
2. Simplify the ratio 12:16 to its lowest terms.

## Topic 1: Odd-One-Out (Word Groups)

**Step 1 — Establish the method: find what MOST of the words have in common first, then identify which one breaks that pattern**

**Worked Example:**
"Rose, Tulip, Sunflower, Carrot" → Test: Rose, Tulip, Sunflower are all flowers. Carrot is a vegetable/root. Odd one out: Carrot.

**Worked Example (a trickier case with more than one possible grouping, testing careful reasoning):**
"Whale, Shark, Dolphin, Salmon" → All live in water, so that's not the distinguishing feature. Test another category: Whale and Dolphin are mammals; Shark and Salmon are fish. This puzzle actually needs the SPECIFIC instruction "which does not belong with the others" to be based on ONE clear odd-one-out — if the intended answer is based on "mammal vs fish," then EITHER Whale/Dolphin (mammals) are the pair that stands apart from Shark/Salmon (fish), OR vice versa, depending on which group is "the odd ones." This shows why reading the exact instruction matters — sometimes there are two valid groupings, and identifying the SPECIFIC contrast intended requires elimination based on the answer options given.

**Practice Questions:**
1. Find the odd one out: "Circle, Square, Triangle, Ball" and explain your reasoning.
2. Find the odd one out: "Doctor, Teacher, Nurse, Hospital" (hint: three are people/professions, one is a place).

## Topic 2: Word Analogies

**Step 1 — Establish the METHOD: identify the RELATIONSHIP between the first pair, then apply the SAME relationship to find the missing word**

**Worked Example (part-to-whole relationship):**
"Finger is to Hand as Toe is to ___?"
Relationship: a finger is PART OF a hand. Applying the same relationship: a toe is part of a ___? Answer: Foot.

**Worked Example (function relationship):**
"Pen is to Write as Knife is to ___?"
Relationship: a pen is USED TO write. A knife is used to ___? Answer: Cut.

**Worked Example (category relationship, a different type — testing that not all analogies use the SAME kind of relationship)**
"Rose is to Flower as Mango is to ___?"
Relationship: a rose is a TYPE OF flower. A mango is a type of ___? Answer: Fruit.

**Practice Questions:**
1. Complete: "Hand is to Glove as Foot is to ___?"
2. Complete: "Bird is to Nest as Bee is to ___?"
3. Complete: "Cold is to Ice as Hot is to ___?" (identify the relationship type first: is this cause/effect, part/whole, or opposite?)

## Topic 3: Word Classification and Grouping

**Step 1 — Establish sorting words into categories by shared properties**

**Worked Example:**
Sort into two groups: "Lion, Rose, Tiger, Sunflower, Elephant, Tulip" → Group 1 (animals): Lion, Tiger, Elephant. Group 2 (flowers): Rose, Sunflower, Tulip.

**Practice Questions:**
1. Sort into two groups: "Apple, Chair, Banana, Table, Orange, Bed" (what are the two categories?).
2. Which word does NOT belong in this group, and why: "Red, Blue, Green, Triangle, Yellow"?

## Topic 4: Basic Coding (Letter/Number Substitution)

**Step 1 — Establish that a "code" follows a FIXED, consistent rule that must be discovered by testing**

**Worked Example:**
"If CAT is coded as DBU, what is DOG coded as?"
Test the rule: C→D (shift forward by 1 letter), A→B (shift by 1), T→U (shift by 1). Consistent rule: shift each letter forward by 1.
Apply to DOG: D→E, O→P, G→H. Coded as: EPH.

**Practice Questions:**
1. If DOG is coded as EPH (using the same "shift forward by 1" rule), what would CAT be coded as? (Check your answer against the worked example above.)
2. If 1=A, 2=B, 3=C... what number represents the letter E?

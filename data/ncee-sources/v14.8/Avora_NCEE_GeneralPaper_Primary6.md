## Part A: Basic Science

### 1. Human Body Systems (Introductory)

**Step 1 — Establish each system by its FUNCTION, not just its name**

The digestive system breaks down food into nutrients the body can use (mouth→stomach→intestines). The respiratory system takes in oxygen and removes carbon dioxide (nose/lungs). The circulatory system moves blood (carrying oxygen and nutrients) around the body via the heart and blood vessels.

**Practice Questions:**
1. Which system breaks down food?
2. Which organ is central to the circulatory system?

### 2. Plants and Animals — Classification Basics

**Worked Example:** Animals can be classified as vertebrates (have a backbone, e.g., fish, birds, mammals) or invertebrates (no backbone, e.g., insects, worms).

**Practice Questions:**
1. Classify as vertebrate or invertebrate: a snake, a butterfly, a dog, a spider.

### 3. Simple Machines and Everyday Technology

**Worked Example:** A lever (like a see-saw) makes lifting easier by using a pivot point. A pulley (like on a well) makes lifting heavy objects easier by changing the direction of force needed.

**Practice Questions:**
1. Name a simple machine used to lift heavy water from a well.
2. Give an example of a lever used in everyday life.

### 4. Energy and Forces (Introductory)

**Worked Example:** Gravity is a force that pulls objects toward the Earth — this is why a dropped ball falls down, not up or sideways.

**Practice Questions:**
1. What force causes a dropped object to fall to the ground?
2. Name one source of renewable energy (e.g., sunlight, wind).

### 5. Health and Hygiene

**Practice Questions:**
1. Name two ways to prevent the spread of germs (e.g., washing hands, covering your mouth when coughing).
2. Why is it important to eat a balanced diet?

## Part B: Social Studies / National Values / Civic Education

### 1. Nigeria's Government Structure (Introductory)

**Step 1 — Establish the three tiers of government by their SCOPE (what area each governs)**

Federal government (governs the whole country), State government (governs one state), Local government (governs a smaller local area within a state).

**Practice Questions:**
1. Name the three tiers of government in Nigeria.
2. Which tier of government would handle a matter affecting the ENTIRE country (e.g., national defense)?

### 2. National Symbols and Their Meaning

**Worked Example:** The Nigerian flag has green-white-green stripes: green represents the nation's rich agriculture/natural wealth, white represents peace and unity.

**Practice Questions:**
1. What do the colors of the Nigerian flag represent?
2. Name Nigeria's national anthem's first line (or describe its general theme of unity/pride).

### 3. Rights and Responsibilities of Citizens

**Practice Questions:**
1. Name one right guaranteed to Nigerian citizens (e.g., freedom of expression, right to education).
2. Name one responsibility citizens have toward their community.

### 4. Nigeria's History — Key Milestones

**Worked Example:** Nigeria gained independence from British colonial rule on October 1, 1960.

**Practice Questions:**
1. In what year did Nigeria gain independence?
2. Name one significant event in Nigeria's history you have learned about.

### 5. Current Affairs and General Knowledge (NCEE-Relevant)

**Practice Questions:**
1. Name the current President of Nigeria (Avora should verify this is current via search when delivering live, since it can change).
2. Name one of Nigeria's neighboring countries.
